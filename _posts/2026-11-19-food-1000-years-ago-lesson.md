I recently read [this book](https://www.amazon.in/Lords-Earth-Sea-History-Empire/dp/9353457939) _Lords Of Earth And Sea - A History Of The Chola Empire_ by Anirudh Kanisetty.

The book covered the rise and fall of the Chola empire and was very interesting. Among other things, it explained how temple construction is mainly a political activity and how inscriptions are propaganda, that too wildly exaggerated. But a couple of things that stood out for me were these.

First, on Page 209 in the chapter called _Death and Taxes_ it gives a description of the meals of the Chalukya emperors.

> The emperor's typical meal consisted of four courses. The first was rice with lentils and ghee; the second, rice with soft meats and broken pulses; the third, rice again, with sour, marinated meats, sprouts and pickles; and finally thick curd mixed with rice, buttermilk and salt.

I was overjoyed at how a 1000 year old royal meal still seems very similar to what I grew up eating. The first and fourth courses were exactly the same as what maternal grandmother used to make every day. I experienced it when I visited Rajahmundry for my summer holidays. It just made my day. The author gets this information from [a different book](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Abrahams-Luggage-Social-Medieval-Connections/dp/1107173884?th=1) called Abraham's Luggage: A Social Life of Things in the Medieval Indian Ocean World (Asian Connections) by Elizabeth A. Lambourn (I have not read that).

Second, in the very next page, the author talks about how "tax-evading gentry" contributed to the decline of the Chola empire and has this to say:

> It might seem strange that a state would allow its own revenue base to be gifted away like this, but medieval states didn't have the same objectives as modern welfare states, which are expected to spend on their citizens' education, healthcare and the like. Medieval states existed primarily to benefit their ruling classes, and only incidentally 'protected' the lower classes as generators of income. So it was not particularly a problem if the poor were getting taxed more, since the upper classes around the Chola emperor were getting richer. Their gods were prospering, and large temple estates were expanding cultivation. To the twelfth-century mind, the flourishing of a landed order favouring the divine and their wealthy devotees simply proved that the Cholas were ideal kings.

I always found it odd when people look back at the past with rose tinted glasses. Raja Raja Cholan is always mentioned in Tamil culture as the pinnacle of our civilization and seen as a great king. But this kind of thinking that glorifies the past always felt disconcerting to me. Shouldn't monarchy be seen as an inherently bad form of government? Living in the UK, I hate the fact that we still have a monarchy. What is it if not a form of caste system, where you are king/queen just because of your bloodline?

Moreover, medieval and ancient kings and queens would have no doubt been tyrants in a majority of cases, as can be seen from dictators today in countries like Russia, China, North Korea etc or leaders with dictatorial tendencies, currently even in democracies like India and the USA. We see that the moment people have a little bit of power they have a tendency to abuse it. Why would it have been any different in the past?

I view the common feeling people have when they look at their own childhood as an ideal time from which we have "degenerated" today, with the same skepticism. To me, the current time is the best time of history so far, with all the medical advances, the scientific knowledge, with the more equitable political systems we have today, the ability to travel the world and the widening of our own experiences such travel brings, the long life expectancy, the improvement in human rights etc. I never would trade this away to living any time in the past. Our present time is not without problems (yes I am aware of climate change, wars, human rights abuses), but I disagree with the notion that things were better in the past. *Relatively* speaking we are mostly better off than we were in the past. I reiterate the word "*relatively*".

So, no thank you, I do not have any interest in living in some past that is supposedly better than today. While I have a lot of worries about where our world is heading, I am happier where I am and when I am than I would have been at any other time in history.
