---

I saw [this petition](https://petition.parliament.uk/petitions/785354) going around today that asks the UK government to require all restaurants to provide non-religiously slaughtered meat options.

I thought about it a bit and realized how ridiculous it is. So I wanted to discuss it a bit.

To get this out of the way, I see this petition as a thinly veiled Islamophobic attempt to target Halal (and probably Kosher foods too, but I think mostly Halal). The petition does try to pre-empt this by saying it is not intended to restrict religious freedom, but I don't buy it (as you will see below).

Also, just to be open, I do not eat meat. I grew up a vegetarian and believe that being vegan is morally superior. I am very "vegan-sympathetic", in that, I would like to be a vegan if I had the self-control and the moral backbone to stick to my principles. I grew up a vegetarian and believe that being vegan is morally superior (yes, I also realize, being vegan is not easy on the wallet and hence not really an option for those who are poor). I do try, by avoiding cow's milk, or avoiding cheese in my sandwiches, avoiding butter to toast my bread etc., but I enjoy Pizza (one of my top three favourite foods) too much to be a "proper" vegan. Anyway, enough of my moral failings. Back to the petition.

First: if people want to eat meat that is not religiously slaughtered, they need to find restaurants that meet their need. If there aren't any around them, then it probably means many people don't care. So why are then then making so much fuss about it? What exactly is the objection? Does that meat taste different? No. Then, is it just that in their mind, they feel this particular meat is religiously slaughtered and so they don't want it? By that logic, are they ok if the food is not slaughtered per religious needs, but was offered to the gods (say as a ritual) before being given to them? Would that also go against ther "freedom of choice"? What if the chefs pray before cooking? Would that also be a problem? See how irrational this whole ask is?

Second: What if I use the logic in the petition and say I want to be able to eat vegan food, and so I need every restaurant to have a vegan variant of every item on their menu. Or what if I demand that every non-veg restaurant also offer food slaughtered according to practice of [Jhatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhatka) followed by some Sikhs? If the petition's logic is to be followed through, there are so many other similar scenarios that would force non-veg restaurants to provide more and more choices. Which is why, I feel, this petition is not really about freedom of choice, but intended to target Islam (or Judaism, but the share of Kosher food is very small in the UK, so more likely that Islam is the target)

Third, there are legitimate concerns about religious slaughter methods, and I [learnt from RSPCA](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/farm/slaughter/religiousslaughter) that while UK law requires animals to be stunned before slaughter, it also provides exceptions for religious needs. If the petition framed the rationale to be based on animal welfare, I would have been sympathetic to the idea, but even then, the ask must have been that religious slaughter methods should not receive exemptions, or be required to be at least as humane as non-religious slaughter methods, not that restaurants provide options for every kind of religious belief. That RSPCA page also tells us that 88% of Halal slaughter anyway stuns animals (which is what non-religious slaughter does too). While we can argue anything less than 100% is not moral, the fact that 88% animals are already stunned, makes it very unlikely that the petition is indeed based on animal welfare.

You might think I am jumping to conclusions when I say this petition is targeting Islam (or Muslims). I guess there is, technically, a theoretical possibility I am wrong on that front, as I cannot read people's minds. But the fact that Halal is the most common religious slaughter method in the UK, makes my conclusion pretty much inescapable.

Realistically, there is no chance that the UK government would take this up in any serious way. A lot of such petitions keep floating around, and so my concern is not that this will be seriously considered. I only discuss it here to highlight an example of bigotry masquerading as "freedom of choice and conscience" and "protecting consumer choice".
